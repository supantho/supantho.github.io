    About

    I’m Supantho Rakshit, a PhD candidate at Princeton University. I work in the Psychology of Language Lab with Adele Goldberg, studying what large language models learn about the structure of human language — and what that, in turn, can tell us about language itself.

    My path here was a little unusual. I started in physics and quantum optics (a B.A. from Columbia and an M.A. in Electrical & Computer Engineering at Princeton, working on quantum error correction and photonic quantum computing), and grew increasingly fascinated by emergent structure — how organized behavior arises from simple underlying rules. That question led me from quantum systems to language models, where I now use tools from mechanistic interpretability, information theory, and construction grammar to ask how linguistic structure emerges inside neural representations.

    I’m also a lifelong lover of languages — I speak eight, and I took first place in the international finals of the Hebrew Language Olympiad. That curiosity about how languages work, differ, and relate to one another is a big part of what drew me to this research in the first place. More on that on my Interests page.
