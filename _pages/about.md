I'm **Supantho Rakshit**, a PhD student at **Princeton University**, where I work with [Adele Goldberg](https://adele.scholar.princeton.edu/). My research sits at the intersection of **linguistics** and **machine learning**: I study what large language models learn about the structure of human language, and what that tells us about language itself.

Most recently I've been asking whether the internal representations of multilingual language models recover the *phylogenetic* structure of language families — the deep historical relationships between languages — without ever being told about them. It turns out they do, and the degree to which a model recovers this structure predicts how well it generalizes across languages. This work was accepted for an **oral presentation at CogSci 2026**.

Outside of research, I'm endlessly curious about **languages and linguistics**, **culture**, and **travel** — see my [Interests](/interests/) page for more of that side of me.

## Research interests

- Representation learning and interpretability in multilingual models
- Computational and historical linguistics
- Cross-lingual generalization and transfer
- What LLMs can (and can't) tell us about human language structure
